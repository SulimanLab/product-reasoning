# Foundations

This file contains the linguistic basis. It is deliberately more important than any list of “bad phrases.”

## 1. Grammatical correctness is not enough

A sentence can:

- use Arabic words;
- obey ordinary grammar;
- contain no foreign spelling;

and still carry the **sentence logic of another language**.

The skill therefore checks two layers:

1. Is the sentence grammatically possible?
2. Is this how Arabic naturally organizes the meaning?

The second question is where translation residue often appears.

## 2. Languages divide meaning differently

Do not assume one source-language unit has one Arabic equivalent.

English may use:

- a preposition where Arabic changes the whole sentence;
- a noun phrase where Arabic prefers a verb;
- an explicit subject where Arabic omits it;
- a phrasal or analytic construction where Arabic morphology carries the meaning;
- a repeated tense marker where Arabic context already settles time.

Translation should transfer **meaning and function**, not the source grammar.

## 3. Arabic has native compression mechanisms

Before adding helper words, inspect Arabic's own tools:

- verbal derivation;
- transitivity patterns;
- comparative/superlative forms;
- attached pronouns;
- particles of condition, restriction, purpose, and exception;
- word order and ellipsis.

This is not a command to use rare morphology. Use the native form only when it remains natural to a modern reader.

## 4. Particles carry meaning, not labels

Do not translate prepositions by dictionary pairing.

`through` does not always equal `من خلال`.
`in case` does not always equal `في حال`.
`when` does not always equal `عندما`.

Ask what relation the source expresses:

- cause?
- instrument?
- location?
- condition?
- time?
- direction?
- opposition?

Then choose the Arabic construction that expresses that relation.

## 5. Natural Arabic may be modern Arabic

This skill does not attempt to recreate medieval prose.

A modern usage can be valid for product writing when it is:

- widely understood;
- precise;
- stable in the field;
- lighter than a forced archaic alternative.

The skill distinguishes **foreign-shaped writing** from **ordinary language change**.

## 6. The unit of judgment is the expression

Do not create a blacklist of isolated words.

The same word may be:

- natural in one context;
- heavy in another;
- a literal translation in a third.

Judge the relation between meaning, construction, and context.

## 7. Rebuild from meaning

The safest repair procedure is:

1. forget the exact source wording;
2. state the intended meaning;
3. write that meaning as Arabic;
4. compare against the source only to verify nothing was lost.

This reverses the usual bad workflow of choosing Arabic words while preserving English structure.
