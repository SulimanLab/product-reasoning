# Product Reasoning

Use product reasoning to prevent screen-first implementation.

## The sequence

### Outcome

What should become easier, clearer, safer, faster, more trustworthy, or more valuable for the user?

Name the change in the user's world, not the artifact being built.

### Decision

What must the user understand, choose, trust, or do next?

Ask:

- Is this decision necessary?
- Who should make it: user, system, or product?
- What information is needed at the moment of decision?
- What uncertainty remains?
- What happens if the choice is wrong?
- Is it reversible?

Remove unnecessary cognitive effort. Preserve meaningful decisions.

### Interaction

Only after the decision is clear, choose the interaction that expresses it naturally.

### Constraints

Balance four lenses:

- **Human:** can the user understand and act?
- **Business:** does this support the intended product outcome?
- **Technology:** can it be built and operated reliably?
- **Risk:** what happens if the product or user gets this wrong?

Do not moralize user versus business. Look for aligned value and surface real conflicts when they exist.

## Product decisions hiding inside implementation requests

“Add a button” may actually mean:

- create a commitment;
- save progress;
- change state;
- trigger payment;
- disclose consent;
- begin a process;
- navigate with no semantic consequence.

The implementation is not the decision. Identify what the action means first.
