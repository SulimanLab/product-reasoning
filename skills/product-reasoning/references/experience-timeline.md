# Experience Timeline

UX exists across time, not only on the rendered screen.

For meaningful actions, inspect the states that can change user understanding or trust:

1. **Before** — what does the user believe will happen?
2. **Action** — what commitment or request did they make?
3. **Feedback** — did the system acknowledge it?
4. **Wait** — is progress or uncertainty understandable?
5. **Success** — what changed, and is it clear?
6. **Partial success** — what completed and what did not?
7. **Failure** — what happened, and what can the user do?
8. **Interruption** — refresh, back, close, network loss, external handoff.
9. **Return** — can the user resume without reconstructing context?
10. **Recovery** — retries, correction, rollback, cancellation.
11. **Aftermath** — what expectation now exists for the next visit?

Do not manufacture edge cases for completeness. Model the states that affect completion, confidence, money, safety, persistence, or recovery.

Implementation completeness is not experience completeness.
