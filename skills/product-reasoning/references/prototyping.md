# Prototyping

A prototype is **throwaway code that answers a product question**.

## When to prototype

Prototype when seeing or interacting with the decision is faster than explaining it, especially for:

- hierarchy;
- progressive disclosure;
- navigation;
- state transitions;
- sequence;
- competing interaction models;
- async/wait/failure behavior.

## Fidelity follows uncertainty

High uncertainty → cheap artifact.

Lower uncertainty → higher fidelity.

Do not polish unresolved decisions.

## Default artifact

For product/UI questions, prefer a small runnable HTML prototype when the environment allows it.

Use realistic content, real hierarchy, meaningful controls, and the states needed to judge the question. Keep decoration, architecture, and animation rough unless they are themselves the question.

## Stop point

Build the smallest artifact that makes the decision inspectable, then stop before production implementation if a consequential product direction remains unresolved.

Once the direction is approved, implement it and stop re-litigating the prototype.
